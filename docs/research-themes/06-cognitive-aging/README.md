# Cognitive Aging & Behavioral Change

## Research Goal

Study long-term behavioral changes that may relate to aging, disease, stress, cognition, or environmental pressure.

Key questions:

- Does an individual show changes in movement, response, feeding, or social behavior?
- Are behavior changes associated with age, injury, disease, or stress?
- Can early warning patterns be detected responsibly?
- Which observations require expert review?

## Traditional Methods

- Long-term field observation.
- Cognitive or behavioral assays in controlled settings.
- Health checks.
- Manual ethograms.
- Individual life-history records.

## Pain Points

- Requires long-term individual records.
- Behavioral signals can be subtle and context-dependent.
- Small sample sizes are common.
- Model outputs can be easy to overinterpret.

## What AI Can Do

- Track changes in movement and activity rhythms.
- Detect deviations from individual baselines.
- Summarize longitudinal behavioral records.
- Combine visual, acoustic, tracking, and environmental evidence.
- Prioritize clips or records for expert review.

## Possible Data

- Individual ID records.
- Video and camera sequences.
- Acoustic data.
- Movement records.
- Health and field notes.
- Environmental context.

## Recommended Techniques

- Longitudinal time-series analysis.
- Anomaly detection.
- Multimodal representation learning.
- Behavior classification.
- Expert-in-the-loop review.

## Recommended Devices

- Camera nodes.
- Acoustic nodes.
- Tracking devices.
- Environmental sensors.
