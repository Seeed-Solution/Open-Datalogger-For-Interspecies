# Acoustic Monitoring & Stress Signals

## Research Goal

Use sound to understand species presence, vocal behavior, communication, stress, and abnormal events.

Key questions:

- Which species or individuals are vocalizing?
- What call type is present?
- Do vocal patterns indicate stress, disturbance, or abnormal behavior?
- How do acoustic patterns change across time, season, and habitat?

## Traditional Methods

- Manual listening and spectrogram review.
- Point-count surveys.
- Field notes from trained observers.
- Dedicated bioacoustic recorders.

## Pain Points

- Audio data volume is large.
- Manual annotation is time-consuming.
- Weather, insects, vehicles, and human activity create noise.
- Calls may vary across region, season, age, and social context.

## What AI Can Do

- Detect sound events in long recordings.
- Classify species or call types.
- Cluster unknown vocalizations for expert review.
- Identify changes in calling rate, frequency, or timing.
- Run lightweight event detection at the edge.

## Possible Data

- Continuous audio recordings.
- Triggered sound clips.
- Spectrograms.
- Weather and time-of-day data.
- Human annotation labels.

## Recommended Techniques

- Audio event detection.
- Spectrogram classification.
- Self-supervised audio embeddings.
- Noise filtering.
- Time-series analysis.

## Recommended Devices

- Acoustic recorder nodes.
- XIAO-based low-power audio devices.
- Edge devices with local storage.
- Weatherproof microphones.
