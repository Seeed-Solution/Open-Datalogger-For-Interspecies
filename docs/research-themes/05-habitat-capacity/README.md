# Habitat Health & Carrying Capacity

## Research Goal

Understand whether habitat conditions and food resources can support a wildlife population over time.

Key questions:

- Are food, water, shelter, and movement corridors available?
- How do environmental conditions affect animal presence and reproduction?
- Are habitat changes creating stress or displacement?
- Which indicators should be monitored in the field?

## Traditional Methods

- Vegetation surveys.
- Water and soil sampling.
- Remote sensing.
- Manual habitat scoring.
- Long-term ecological monitoring.

## Pain Points

- Habitat data is often collected separately from animal observations.
- Sensor maintenance can be difficult in remote areas.
- Environmental effects may be delayed or indirect.
- Site-level variation makes generalization hard.

## What AI Can Do

- Fuse animal observations with environmental time series.
- Detect habitat changes from images or remote sensing.
- Model relationships between habitat variables and animal behavior.
- Identify early warning signals.
- Summarize long-term trends for conservation planning.

## Possible Data

- Temperature, humidity, rainfall, water level, soil, and vegetation data.
- Camera observations.
- Satellite or drone imagery.
- Animal movement and presence records.
- Field survey notes.

## Recommended Techniques

- Time-series analysis.
- Sensor fusion.
- Geospatial modeling.
- Change detection.
- Forecasting.

## Recommended Devices

- Environmental sensors.
- Camera nodes.
- LoRa sensor networks.
- Weather stations.
- Remote sensing sources.
