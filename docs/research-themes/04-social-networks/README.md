# Social Behavior Networks

## Research Goal

Understand how individuals interact, associate, form groups, and change relationships over time.

Key questions:

- Which individuals are frequently observed together?
- What does group structure look like?
- Are there stable families, dominance patterns, or social roles?
- How do social relationships change with season, habitat, or disturbance?

## Traditional Methods

- Direct field observation.
- Radio telemetry.
- Manual group composition records.
- Long-term individual identification.

## Pain Points

- Social interactions require accurate individual IDs.
- Field observations are sparse and biased toward visible locations.
- Multi-animal scenes are hard to annotate.
- Association does not always mean interaction.

## What AI Can Do

- Track multiple individuals in video.
- Combine re-identification with co-occurrence records.
- Build association graphs.
- Detect grooming, aggression, mating, feeding, or group movement events.
- Summarize long-term social changes.

## Possible Data

- Video clips.
- Camera trap sequences.
- Tracking records.
- Individual ID records.
- Field notes.

## Recommended Techniques

- Multi-object tracking.
- Pose estimation when appropriate.
- Individual re-identification.
- Graph analysis.
- Behavior classification.

## Recommended Devices

- Video-capable camera nodes.
- reCamera.
- GPS or LoRa trackers.
- Fixed observation stations.
